## Customer Insights

### Customer Behavior Patterns

Gen Z and modern social media users are moving away from overly polished, heavily filtered images towards **"Authenticity" and "Lo-Fi" aesthetics**.

- **Trend Shift**: Preference for "candid," "blurred," and "in-the-moment" vibes over static perfection.
- **Platform Usage**: TikTok and Instagram are used primarily as discovery engines for styles and apps. If an app goes viral on TikTok for a specific "look," downloads spike immediately.
- **Short-Form Influence**: Photography habits are influenced by video trends; users are accustomed to dynamic movement rather than rigid posing.

_Source: [Vertex AI Search - Gen Z trends 2024-2025]_

### Pain Points and Challenges

The primary barrier to good photography is not equipment, but **subject confidence and physical awkwardness**.

- **"What do I do with my hands?"**: A universal struggle. Subjects feel physically awkward and stiff without guidance.
- **Mirror vs. Lens Disconnect**: Users feel they look good in the mirror but bad in photos due to lens distortion and lack of posing awareness.
- **Fear of Judgment**: Public photography creates anxiety (scopophobia), leading to rushed and poor-quality shots.

_Source: [Vertex AI Search - Photography pain points]_

### Decision-Making Processes

- **Discovery**: Driven by social proof (Influencers, TikTok trends).
- **Trial Phase**: High sensitivity to UX friction. If the "Magic" doesn't happen in the first 30 seconds (or if ads intrude), the app is uninstalled.
- **Retention**: Users keep apps that solve a specific problem (e.g., "The app that makes my legs look long" or "The app for vintage film vibes").

_Source: [Vertex AI Search - App uninstallation triggers]_

### Customer Satisfaction Drivers

- **Effortless Coaching**: Users value guidance that feels natural, not rigid.
- **Privacy Assurance**: Growing concern over AI utilizing personal biometrics.
- **Instant Rewards**: The result must look immediately "better" (not necessarily "edited," but "better composition/lighting") on the screen _before_ the shutter is pressed.
